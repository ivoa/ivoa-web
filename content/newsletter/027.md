**IVOA Newsletter Editors:** Deborah Baines, Thomas Boch.

**The International Virtual Observatory Alliance (IVOA) was formed in June 2002 with a mission to facilitate the international coordination and collaboration necessary for the development and deployment of the tools, systems and organizational structures necessary to enable the international utilization of astronomical archives as an integrated and interoperating virtual observatory.
The IVOA now comprises 20 VO programs from Argentina, Armenia, Australia, Brazil, Canada, Chile, China, Europe, France, Germany, Hungary, India, Italy, Japan, Russia, South Africa, Spain, Ukraine, the United Kingdom, and the United States and an inter-governmental organization (ESA). Membership is open to other national and international programs according to the [IVOA Guidelines for Participation](https://ivoa.net/Documents/latest/IVOAParticipation.html).
You can read more about the IVOA and what we do at [https://ivoa.net/about/](/about/).**

**What is the VO?**

The Virtual Observatory (VO) aims to provide a research environment that will open up new possibilities for scientific research based on data discovery, efficient data access, and interoperability. The vision is of global astronomy archives connected via the VO to form a multiwavelength digital sky that can be searched, visualized, and analyzed in new and innovative ways. 
VO projects worldwide working toward this vision are already providing science capabilities with new tools and services. 
This newsletter, aimed at astronomers, highlights VO tools and technologies for doing astronomy research, recent papers, and upcoming events.

## Reflections from Strasbourg {#reflections-strasbourg}

JJ Kavelaars

The June 2026 IVOA Interoperability Meeting in Strasbourg was a great success, bringing together 153 participants—117 in person and 36 online. It was particularly encouraging to see so many people attending their first InterOp. The mix of new participants and long-standing colleagues led to lively discussions, new contacts and, I hope, collaborations that will continue well beyond the meeting.

{{</ side-image >}}

<div style="height:0.5em;"></div>

Our thanks go to VO-France and the local organizing team, including Ada Nebot, Anaïs Oberto, Thomas Keller and Céline Halter, as well as the many others who contributed. The meeting was exceptionally well organized, from the venue and hybrid arrangements to the social programme. That welcoming environment made it easy for people to meet, exchange ideas and become involved.

Artificial intelligence was a strong theme, both in the dedicated plenary and across several other sessions. The discussions highlighted exciting new capabilities, but also reinforced the importance of the less visible foundations: discoverable data, reliable metadata, common formats, semantics and provenance. As AI becomes more widely used in astronomy, the IVOA’s work becomes more—not less—important. These technologies will be most useful when they operate across well-described, interoperable, and trustworthy data collections.

The InterOp also reminded us that the IVOA is sustained by colleagues who volunteer their time and expertise. Thank you to everyone who agreed to serve as a chair or vice-chair of a Working Group, Interest Group or committee, and to those completing their terms. Organizing discussions, building consensus, guiding documents and welcoming newcomers are what allow the Alliance to function.

With new observatories and missions beginning operations or moving through development, interoperability will be essential to realizing their long-term scientific value. Strasbourg showed that our community has the ideas, expertise and enthusiasm needed for that work.

The IVOA is the foundation of our operational services and raising the visibility of that fact is a goal of the Exec. I hope we will start to see banners that make this clear to our users visible on the sites of our member organizations.  We really are: Built on IVOA standards and FAIR principles.

Thank you to everyone who helped make the meeting such a productive and enjoyable week.

## Advancing Virtual Observatory Standards for High-Energy Astrophysics {#high-energy-astrophysics}

Janet Evans, Ian Evans, Bruno Khelifi, Mathieu Servillat and the HEA Interest group

High-energy astrophysics (HEA) presents unique challenges for data discovery and interoperability within the Virtual Observatory (VO). Unlike optical, infrared, or radio astronomy, HEA observations are based on the detection of individual particles such as X-rays, gamma rays, cosmic rays, and neutrinos. As HEA observatories increasingly provide science-ready data products, VO standards must evolve to support these specialized datasets.

{{</ side-image >}}

<div style="height:0.5em;"></div>

A recent note from the IVOA High Energy Interest Group (HEIG) proposes a series of enhancements to the Observation Core Components (ObsCore) data model together with a HEA-specific ObsCore extension. Central to the proposal is the recognition that HEA observations are fundamentally based on event lists — collections of detected particle events characterized by properties such as time, direction, and energy. Scientific analysis also requires Instrument Response Functions (IRFs), which describe how detectors transform incoming particles into measured signals. Because event lists and IRFs are closely linked, both should be represented within VO discovery services.

To support this, the proposal introduces two new data product categories: "hea-event-list" and "hea-event-bundle." The latter combines an event list with the response functions and/or ancillary products needed for analysis. The note also recommends new categories for advanced scientific products, including probability density functions, statistical samples or draws, response functions, and region descriptions, making these increasingly important analysis products discoverable through standard VO queries.

The proposal further addresses limitations in existing metadata. HEA datasets often contain partially calibrated information, making current calibration levels inadequate. The authors recommend describing calibration status separately for spatial, spectral, and temporal axes. Additional metadata are proposed to better characterize energy-dependent instrument performance, detailed observation time coverage, and energy ranges using units familiar to HEA researchers.
Several new metadata fields are also suggested to describe observing modes, tracking and scanning strategies, data-processing methods, event classifications, and the type of messenger particle detected. These additions will improve data discovery and support the growing field of multi-messenger astronomy.

Together, these recommendations represent an important step toward integrating HEA data more fully into the Virtual Observatory ecosystem. Many of the proposed enhancements will benefit not only high-energy astronomy but also other disciplines that rely on advanced data products and complex observational metadata. Along with a similar ObsCore extension proposal from the Radio Interest Group, this work provides valuable input to ongoing IVOA efforts to expand ObsCore and develop the semantic standards needed for current and next-generation astronomical archives.

A Plenary session at the June 2026 IVOA Interop highlighted key points of the proposal and demonstrated three prototype reference implementations (X-Ray, Gamma ray, and Neutrinos).  Discussions with the Semantics working group and Data Model working group will be ongoing.  The upgrade is planned for Obscore 1.2.

For more information, please consult the [IVOA Roadmap to an ObsCore Extension and Discovery of High Energy Astrophysics Data](https://github.com/ivoa/HighEnergyObsCoreExt?tab=readme-ov-file). Information about the HEA Interest Group (HEIG) is available on their [web page](https://wiki.ivoa.net/twiki/bin/view/IVOA/HEGroup), and interested community members are encouraged to contact the group or join the email list at heig 'at' ivoa.net.

### HiPS3D New Tools and Datasets {#hips3d}

Thomas Boch, Pierre Fernique, Matthieu Baumann, Sébastien Derriere 

The [Hierarchical Progressive Survey (HiPS)](https://www.ivoa.net/documents/HiPS) standard has dramatically improved accessibility to very large astronomical image surveys in the past decade. The Applications Working Group is now starting the process to expand the HiPS paradigm to cubic data, with the definition of HiPS3D. This new standard will enable the efficient distribution of volumetric astronomical data, moving beyond traditional 2D imaging to support complex cubes such as spectroscopic surveys.

The Hipsgen tool now provides the capability to easily create HiPS3D surveys from a collection of individual FITS cubes. Several data collections have been processed and are already available, for example the CALIFA DR3, MaNGA DR17, and even the MUSE observations (resulting in a 48TB HiPS).

{{</ side-image >}}

<div style="height:0.5em;"></div>

If you are interested in this topic, we recommend reading the [HiPS3D discovery tutorial](https://aladin.cds.unistra.fr/java/TutoHiPS3Den.pdf) written by Pierre Fernique.

Both Aladin Desktop and Aladin Lite have been updated to natively visualize HiPS3D content. Users can now seamlessly navigate through spatial and spectral dimensions, and can even extract resampled FITS cubes around regions of interest from HiPS3D datasets with the HIPS2FITS3D service.

**More information:** [https://aladin.cds.unistra.fr/hips/](https://aladin.cds.unistra.fr/hips/)

### J-PAS EDR and J-PLUS DR4 Released in the Virtual Observatory {#jpas-jplus}

Tamara Civera

J-PAS EDR, the first public release of J-PAS data, together with J-PLUS DR4, are now available through standard Virtual Observatory (VO) services operated and published by the [Centro de Estudios de Física del Cosmos de Aragón (CEFCA)](https://www.cefca.es), expanding the set of large-area optical surveys accessible through the VO ecosystem.

Both surveys are conducted from the [Observatorio Astrofísico de Javalambre (OAJ)](https://oajweb.cefca.es), Teruel, Spain. [J-PAS](https://www.j-pas.org) is a wide-field survey based on 57 optical filters designed to provide low-resolution spectral energy distributions and precise photometric redshifts. [J-PLUS](https://www.j-plus.es) is a multi-band photometric survey providing wide sky coverage in 12 optical bands.

{{</ side-image >}}

<div style="height:0.5em;"></div>

The data are published through the CEFCA Catalogues Portal and exposed via standard VO services, including TAP, SCS, SIAP, and HiPS. These services are published by the CEFCA Publishing Registry and can be accessed directly from any VO-compliant tool. In addition, the CEFCA Catalogues Portal provides web-based interfaces for data exploration, visualization, and download.

J-PAS EDR provides calibrated images and catalogues over 17 square degrees, including approximately 800,000 sources and photometric redshift estimates for nearly 500,000 objects. J-PLUS DR4 covers almost 5,000 square degrees and contains photometric measurements for nearly 97 million sources, with photometric redshifts for more than 79 million objects.

**More information and access to the services:** [https://archive.cefca.es](https://archive.cefca.es)

### Astro-COLIBRI launches the Simple Cone Search {#astro-colibri}

Ilja Jaroschewski

The real-time astrophysics platform Astro-COLIBRI has integrated a fully compliant IVOA Simple Cone Search (SCS) service into the VO registry. Astro-COLIBRI aggregates real-time astronomical alerts of transient events like supernovae, gamma-ray bursts, neutrinos, and gravitational waves from multiple global streams (e.g. GCN, TNS, CBAT, etc.). 

{{</ side-image >}}

<div style="height:0.5em;"></div>

With the new SCS endpoint, users can now seamlessly query recent and archival transients from the past year directly within VO-compliant tools like Aladin and TOPCAT. Using standard position and radius parameters, researchers receive instant, up-to-date transient metadata to enhance their multi-messenger workflows.

**Learn more:** [https://astro-colibri.science](https://astro-colibri.science)

### New Data and Features in ESASky {#esasky}

Henrik Norman, Philip Matsson, Erik Mellegård, Javier Espinosa, Deborah Baines, Marcos López Caniego, Elena Puga 

ESASky continues to evolve as a science-driven discovery portal, providing astronomers with seamless access to data from major space- and ground-based observatories through a single web interface. Recent updates have added new content, including the PLATO Input Catalogue, the 5XMM-DR15 XMM-Newton serendipitous source catalogue, INTEGRAL science products, and the Euclid Q2 outreach HiPS, further expanding the range of resources available for multi-wavelength research.

{{</ side-image >}}

<div style="height:0.5em;"></div>

Alongside these new datasets, ESASky continues to enhance the user experience with new functionality for exploring and visualising astronomical data. Recent additions include phase-folding in the Time-Series Viewer, improved FITS visualisation with Aladin Lite 3, and the ability to send an active ESASky session directly to an ESA Datalabs Jupyter notebook. A beta version of ESASky is also available, giving the community early access to upcoming features, including improved interoperability with other VO-enabled tools. The ESASky team welcomes feedback from users to help shape future development.

Explore ESASky at [https://sky.esa.int](https://sky.esa.int) and try the beta version at [https://sky.esa.int/versions/8beta/](https://sky.esa.int/versions/8beta/)

# Some recent papers about VO-enabled science

### Featured Science Publication {#featured-science-publication}

_The Stripped-star Ultraviolet Magellanic Cloud Survey (SUMS): The Ultraviolet Photometric Catalog and Stripped-star Candidate Selection_

**Ludwig, B. et al.**

_The Astrophysical Journal, Volume 999, 73_

Most massive stars (∼8-25M⊙) interact with a binary companion during their lifetimes. These interactions can remove the hydrogen-rich envelope, producing intermediate-mass (∼2-8M⊙) and helium-rich stars. These "stripped stars" are predicted to emit predominantly in the ultraviolet (UV) and can therefore be identified via a UV excess—provided they are not outshone by their companion. However, despite their importance to binary evolution, supernovae, and ionizing feedback, few stripped stars have been confirmed. This is likely due to the scarcity of wide-field, high angular-resolution, UV surveys of stellar populations with reliable distances and extinction estimates. To address this, we present the Stripped-Star Ultraviolet Magellanic Clouds Survey catalog. We use the Tractor forward modeling software to perform point-spread function photometry on 2420 Swift UVOT images of the LMC and SMC. The resulting catalog contains 734,862 sources in three UV filters to a depth of ∼20 Vega mag. We perform validation tests on the photometry pipeline and highlight the catalog's broad applicability. We then identify sources with excess UV light compared to main-sequence stars and apply a series of quality cuts. From this, we identify 522 candidate stripped stars in the LMC and 298 in the SMC. We assess the potential contamination from other UV excess systems and argue the dominant uncertainty to be dust: early main-sequence stars can mimic the colors of stripped-star binaries when extinction is overcorrected. This survey lays the groundwork for the first systematic census of stripped stars and opens new windows into binary evolution and massive star populations.

DOI: [10.3847/1538-4357/ae2d13](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ae2d13)

# VO calendar {#vo-calendar}

* **1 - 5 November 2026 - ADASS XXXVI**

  (Perth, Australia)

  This annual Astronomical Data Analysis Software and Systems (ADASS) conference, held in a different location each year, is a forum for astronomers, computer scientists, software engineers, faculty members and students working in areas related to algorithms, software and systems for the acquisition, reduction, analysis, and dissemination of astronomical data. The ADASS XXXVI program will include invited talks, contributed papers, display sessions, tutorials, computer demonstrations, and special interest ("Birds of a Feather" or BoF) meetings.

* **6 - 8 November 2026 - IVOA Interoperability Meeting**

  (Perth, Australia)

  The International Virtual Observatory Alliance (IVOA) semi-annual Interoperability meetings provide an opportunity for discussion and development of virtual observatory standards and VO-based applications, and are open to those with an interest in utilizing the VO infrastructure and tools in support of observatory operations and/or astronomical research. 
  The Northern Fall 2026 IVOA Interoperability meeting will follow ADASS and will be held in Perth Australia. Stay informed by visiting the [IVOA Events page](https://wiki.ivoa.net/twiki/bin/view/IVOA/IvoaEvents). 


* **10 - 14 January 2027 - 249th AAS Meeting**

  (Salt Lake City, UT, USA)

  The American Astronomical Society (AAS) meetings serve as a venue for the general astronomical community to gather and discuss the latest science, tools, and technologies in astronomy. VO-related sessions and exhibits will be held during the meeting, including presentations from international VO partners, open to all astronomers. 
  IVOA affiliated institutions will have booths in the exhibition hall throughout the week, demonstrating tools and services such as the NASA Astrophysics Data System, SciServer, VizieR, and other VO data access interfaces. 
  Attendees may learn more about the data and services provided by those institutions, and have face-to-face discussions with developers.
